# Data Card

GigBid uses **real** public job data (Kaggle) and **synthetic** data (personas, hidden client
preferences, planted cases). The two are always kept apart: every record carries
`provenance: real` or `provenance: synthetic`, and real data lives under `data/real/` and
`data/raw/`, synthetic data under `data/personas/` and the planted parts of `evals/datasets/`.

Counts below come from `docs/data_preprocessing_log.md` (generated by
`scripts/data/build_real_jobs.py`) and `docs/notes/raw_inspection.md` (generated by
`scripts/data/inspect_raw.py`). Re-run `python -m scripts.data.build_real_jobs` and
`python -m scripts.data.build_priors` to regenerate everything.

## 2. Preprocessing (real job pipeline)

Script: `scripts/data/build_real_jobs.py`. Rules in `data/real/taxonomy.yaml`.

| Step | Count |
|---|---|
| Raw rows loaded (60k set + 50k set) | 63,950 + 53,058 |
| Dropped: description missing or under 60 characters | 1,239 |
| Dropped: duplicate job URL (query string removed) | 2,343 |
| Dropped: exact duplicate of normalised title + description | 1,835 |
| Dropped: near duplicate (same normalised title and first 300 description characters) | 2,199 |
| Budgets set to null (outside fixed 5 to 100,000 USD or hourly 3 to 300 USD) | 183 |
| Rows where an email, URL, phone number or messenger handle was masked | 12,243 |
| **Cleaned jobs** (`data/real/jobs_clean.parquet`, git ignored) | **109,391** (58,435 from the 60k set, 50,956 from the 50k set) |
| **Job stream** (`data/real/job_stream_1500.parquet`, committed) | **1,500**, seed 4016 |

Normalised schema: `job_id, title, description, skills, category, budget_type, budget_min,
budget_max, posted_date, period, client_country, client_spend, client_payment_verified,
client_rating, client_feedback_count, connects_cost, applicants, experience_level,
source_dataset, provenance`.

* **HTML entities** (`&amp;`, `&quot;`) are decoded.
* **PII masking:** emails → `[EMAIL]`, URLs → `[URL]`, phone numbers with 9 to 15 digits →
  `[PHONE]`, WhatsApp/Telegram/Skype/WeChat handles → `[HANDLE]`. Budgets, years and ranges
  are kept (unit tested). Job URLs are used only as dedupe keys and are not stored.
* **Skills:** the 60k set has up to nine skill tags per job. Tags that appear in at least 20
  jobs (1,306 skills) form `data/real/skill_vocab.json`. For the 50k set, which has no tags,
  vocabulary skills are found in the title and description by word n-gram lookup.
* **Category:** weighted keyword rules (title ×3, skills ×2, description ×1) over 9 categories
  plus `other`. Cleaned counts: web_dev 30,087, marketing 29,879, design 12,641, other 9,597,
  writing 6,036, data_analytics 5,669, mobile_dev 5,453, admin 4,495, video 3,418, ai_ml 2,116.
* **Stream sampling:** stratified by category. The three persona categories (ai_ml,
  data_analytics, web_dev) get a floor of 220 jobs each so every persona has enough relevant
  jobs; the rest are proportional with a minimum of 40. This over-represents AI/ML jobs
  compared with the real market (2% of cleaned jobs, 15% of the stream).
* **Committed outputs:** `job_stream_1500.parquet` (800 KB, masked, only from datasets 1
  and 3, both licensed for redistribution with attribution), `job_stream_sample.json` (first
  200 stream rows), `skill_vocab.json`, `price_stats.json`, `outcome_priors.json`,
  `scam_lexicon.json`, `taxonomy.yaml`, `NOTICE.md`. `jobs_clean.parquet` (54 MB) is git
  ignored; anything over 5 MB never goes into git.

### Price statistics (`data/real/price_stats.json`)

* **Asking prices** (what clients posted) from datasets 1, 2 and 3, after the same budget
  range filter. Percentiles p10, p25, p50, p75, p90 by category, by budget type and by skill
  (400 skills), only when n ≥ 20. Hourly values are the midpoint of the posted range. 613,717
  of the 733,128 unique links in dataset 2 have a usable budget; their category comes from
  the title only.
* **Paid prices** from dataset 4: hourly rates paid and total paid on fixed contracts, by
  category (from the contract title).
* **Connects** versus posted fixed budget, from dataset 3 (the only set with both).
* The fair price band used for gold labels is p25 to p75 of the job's category and budget type.

### Outcome priors (`data/real/outcome_priors.json`, `scripts/data/build_priors.py`)

* **Competition:** share of jobs per applicants bucket and mean applicant count per category
  (dataset 3). GigSim's base reply rate is `reply_scale / applicants`.
* **Client population:** payment verified rate, spend, rating, feedback count, countries
  (dataset 3). GigSim samples its synthetic clients' public history from these.
* **Freelancer side:** success rate, repeat client rate, response time, rates by category
  (dataset 5). Weak prior only, see limitations.
* **Contracts:** open versus closed share, hours and amounts paid (dataset 4).

### Scam lexicon (`data/real/scam_lexicon.json`)

Smoothed log odds of words and word pairs in fraudulent versus legitimate rows of dataset 6,
after dropping duplicate texts.

## 3. Synthetic data

| What | Where | How it is made |
|---|---|---|
| GigSim clients (600) | in memory, `gigsim/world.py` | public history sampled from real distributions; 3% scam clients |
| Hidden client preferences | in memory, never served | per job: 1 to 3 must-have skills from the job's own tags, budget ceiling 0.9 to 1.3 × posted budget, 70% dislike generic openings |
| Live planted jobs | GigSim live stream | templates in `gigsim/templates.yaml`: near duplicates, scams, injected instructions |
| Personas, portfolios, past proposals, dev eval set | `data/personas/`, `evals/datasets/` | `scripts/data/build_personas.py` (seed 4016), see section 4 |

## 4. Limitations

* **Asking, not paid:** budgets in job posts are what clients asked for, not what was paid.
  Paid prices from dataset 4 are reported separately for that reason.
* **Stale and narrow:** postings are from Feb–Mar 2023 (dataset 3) and Dec 2023 – Feb 2024
  (dataset 1). Dataset 3 covers only four search keywords (Developer, Marketing, 3D, Data
  science), and its 3D jobs have no budget. Prices and skills move fast in AI work.
* **No outcomes:** no dataset contains proposals, replies or hires. All reply and hire
  behaviour in GigSim is designed, not learned. GigSim results only mean something relative
  to other GigSim runs.
* **Dataset 5 looks synthetic** (smooth distributions, sequential ids `FL_000000…`). Its
  priors are labelled as weak in the output file.
* **Dataset 6's fraud rows are machine-generated text.** All of them follow one template
  ("earn 5000week immediate hiring contact now at …") padded with random words. After that
  template, the strongest "fraud" words are random fillers (for example "brother", "democrat").
  The lexicon is therefore narrow, and GigBid does not rely on it. Planted scams in GigSim
  and the dev set use hand written patterns that look like real scams (free trial work,
  off platform payment, deposits, requests for ID or bank details).
* **Category rules are keyword based.** Spot checks show sensible titles per category, with
  some noise: for example, jobs that mention dashboards or KPIs in passing can land in
  data_analytics.
* **Scraped text** may contain the original posters' writing quirks and leftover PII that the
  masks miss (for example names). Masking is regex based.
* **Dataset 6 has no stated licence.** Only aggregate word statistics derived from it are
  committed (`scam_lexicon.json`); no rows or text passages.


## 4. Personas, portfolios and evaluation sets (synthetic)

Built by `python -m scripts.data.build_personas` from hand-written project facts in
`scripts/data/persona_specs.py` (seed 4016, fully deterministic). All records carry
`provenance: synthetic`, except dev/held-out jobs taken from the real job stream, which keep
`provenance: real` and only gain gold labels.

**Corpora** (`data/personas/<persona>/`): `profile.json`, `cv.md`, 16 case studies with
front-matter metadata (project, skills, date, client industry), repos (README + code) for
every second project, client reviews for about 70% of projects, and 150 past proposals with
outcomes. The corpus is messy on purpose:
* each project is described three ways (case study, terse CV line, technical README);
* some skills appear only in code (`# uses Celery`), never in prose;
* past proposals carry `won / replied / ignored` outcomes that depend on whether the letter
  was specific and cited a real project, not on its wording.

**Evaluation sets** (`evals/datasets/dev_<persona>.jsonl`; held-out in `data/heldout/`, git
ignored, disjoint jobs):

| Persona | Projects | Dev jobs | Gold bid | Planted scams | Planted injections | Jobs with gold evidence | Held-out jobs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ai_ml | 16 | 198 | 41 | 10 | 10 | 53 | 113 |
| fullstack | 16 | 198 | 35 | 10 | 10 | 62 | 113 |
| data_analyst | 16 | 198 | 41 | 10 | 10 | 62 | 113 |

Source: `evals/datasets/SUMMARY.json`. Each dev set mixes 120 jobs from the persona's
categories, 50 from other categories, 10 planted scams (GigSim templates plus five
hand-written patterns: unpaid trials, deposits, ID/bank requests, crypto off-platform,
equity-only), 10 planted prompt injections appended to relevant jobs, and 8 near-duplicates.

**How gold labels were made (and their limits).** Labels are rule-derived, not human:
* `gold_projects`: projects whose skills (including code-only skills) overlap the job text
  or listed skills in at least two places;
* `gold_decision = bid` when the job is in the persona's categories, not a scam or
  duplicate, has at least two persona skill hits, at least one gold project, and a budget not
  far below the persona's floor;
* `fair_band`: p25 to p75 of real prices for the job's category and budget type
  (`data/real/price_stats.json`).
Because GigBid's own matcher also uses skill overlap, decision metrics on this set are
optimistic about lexical matching and pessimistic about semantic matches the rules miss. The
synthetic past proposals cite the project with the highest skill overlap, which favours
outcome-aware ranking (V4); see the threats to validity in the README.
